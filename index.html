02 • The Silent Side of Communication

What Is Body Language?

Body language means communicating feelings and ideas without using words. Even when we are quiet, our body communicates.

Face

Our expressions can show how we feel—joy, surprise, focus, or concern.

Hands

Our gestures support what we say and help explain ideas clearly.

Posture

The way we stand or sit sends strong signals about confidence and interest.

03 • A Number Worth Understanding

The Often-Quoted 55%

55%

In psychologist Albert Mehrabian’s 1967 study on communicating feelings and attitudes, facial expressions and visual cues played a major role.

Important: 55% is not a strict rule for every conversation or every message.

06 • Signals We Use Every Day

Four Pillars of Body Language

1. Facial Expressions

A smile, frown, or raised eyebrow can communicate emotion instantly.

2. Eye Contact

Looking at someone appropriately shows attention, respect, and interest.

3. Posture

The way we stand or sit shapes how confident and engaged we appear.

4. Hand Gestures

Our hands help explain, emphasize, or illustrate an idea naturally.

07 • How a Message Feels

Open & Closed Signals

Open Body Language

  • Relaxed arms
  • Comfortable eye contact
  • A natural smile
  • Upright, comfortable posture

Helps us seem welcoming and ready to listen.

Closed Body Language

  • Arms folded tightly
  • Looking down or away
  • Turning away from others

May suggest discomfort or disinterest, though context always matters.

08 • Calm Confidence

Confidence in Class

You do not have to be the loudest person in the room to communicate confidently.

Stand Steadily

Keep both feet comfortably on the floor.

Speak Clearly

Use a pace and volume that others can hear.

Take Your Time

Pause and breathe if you feel nervous. Confidence can be calm.

09 • Two-Way Communication

Listening With Your Body

Listening is not just waiting for your turn to speak. Show someone you are listening:

1

Look

Give comfortable attention to the speaker.

2

Nod

Use a small nod when it feels natural.

3

Focus

Put distractions away and listen to understand.

10 • A Thought to Remember

Hearing What Isn’t Said

“The most important thing in communication is hearing what isn’t said.” — Often attributed to Peter Drucker
